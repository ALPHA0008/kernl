# Kernl — Design Partner Master Plan (0 → Partners → Paying → Seed)

**Prepared:** 2026-07-23 · Fourth doc in the strategy series; builds on `MARKET_VALIDATION.md` (market/ACV), `FOUNDER_PLAYBOOK.md` (GTM/product/roadmap), `FOUNDER_BRAND_GTM.md` (brand/outreach base). **Nothing repeated; only execution.**
**Mission window:** 90–180 days. Target ladder: **3–5 design partners → 1–3 paying → repeatable founder-led motion → seed-ready.**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The entire plan runs on one exchange: **you give a free, zero-risk diagnostic of their decision chaos (shadow mode + white-glove policy encoding + the Leakage Report); they give you learning, proof, and a conversion path.** Everything else — accounts, sequences, demos, pilots, pricing — is machinery around that exchange. The Tier-1 list is companies with *public evidence* of AI-agent adoption (Decagon/Fin case-study rosters); the first call happens this week; the first paying conversion is targeted for day ~120; seed conversations open at 3 partners + 1 paying + one audit-grade case study.

## 2. Phase 1 — Design Partner Strategy

**Why would anyone do this?** Honest answer, in their priority order:
1. **They get the Leakage Report free** — a quantified diagnostic of refund/credit/discount inconsistency on *their own history*. Consulting firms charge $20K+ for worse. They keep it even if they never buy.
2. **Zero risk** — shadow mode: read-only ingestion, no workflow change, no agent behavior change, cancel anytime, data deleted on exit.
3. **White-glove encoding** — *you* author their policies into Kernl (turns your biggest adoption risk — authoring burden — into your onboarding service; you learn more from encoding their policies than any interview could teach).
4. **Agent-rollout cover** — a board/CFO-ready answer to "how do we govern what the AI agent decides?" during their live agent deployment.
5. **Roadmap power + price lock** — their domain ships first; year-1 pricing locked at 50% of list.

**Why risk a pre-seed vendor?** Because shadow mode means there is nothing to rip out, the deliverable (report) has standalone value, and the vendor does the work. The honest pitch: *"Worst case, you get a free audit of your decision consistency and delete us. Best case, you never fail an agent audit."*

### Program design

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Cohort | **Max 5** (scarcity is real: white-glove encoding caps founder capacity) |
| Duration | **90-day shadow phase**, then decision point; 6-month total program |
| Price | Free for 90 days → **$750/mo (50% of $1.5K Team list) for year 1** on conversion. Never free beyond day 90 — free forever = worthless signal |
| Their commitments | Data connection (Zendesk/Intercom export or API), 45-min weekly call, policy Q&A access to their ops lead, case-study rights (anonymized OK), intro to their finance lead by week 8 |
| Your deliverables | Encoded policy bundle (their policies, evidence-cited), Leakage Report (day ~30), replay report on ≥1 real policy change (day ~60), audit-trail artifact pack (day ~90) |
| Cadence | Weekly 45-min (first month), bi-weekly after; shared Slack channel; <24h response SLA |
| Success metrics | ≥90% domain decision coverage in shadow ≤30 days · divergence rate quantified · ≥1 replay-gated policy decision · finance/audit stakeholder has seen the ledger |
| Legal | 3-page Design Partner Agreement (§18): mutual confidentiality clause *inside* the agreement (skip standalone NDA theater), DPA attached, IP: your product yours / their data theirs / anonymized aggregate learnings usable, no exclusivity, 30-day termination, **pre-agreed conversion terms** (the $750/mo option expires at day 120 — real deadline, kindly enforced) |

- **Geography:** 1. US · 2. UK/EU-English (EU AI Act narrative bonus) · 3. CA/ANZ.
- **AI maturity:** 1. **Agent live in production 3–12 months** (feels the governance gap NOW — the trigger) · 2. Agent in pilot · 3. Evaluating agents (discovery-only value) · 4. No agent plans (skip — even though Kernl works for humans, the urgency wedge is the agent).

- **Willingness to experiment:** product-led tech companies (Notion/Clay archetype) > D2C subscription ops > traditional mid-market.
- **Budget:** RevOps/Support-Ops tooling budgets exist at 200+ employees; below that it's founder discretionary.
- **Regulatory pressure:** fintech-adjacent > EU-exposed > US B2B SaaS (indirect only — per MARKET_VALIDATION §8, refunds aren't Annex III; don't oversell).

**Primary ICP** (full profile — this is the sharpened version of FOUNDER_PLAYBOOK §4): US B2B SaaS or subscription company, 200–1,000 employees, $30–250M revenue, Zendesk or Intercom stack, **AI support agent live (Decagon/Fin/Sierra-class) or in pilot**, support org 15–80 seats, named Support-Ops/RevOps owner. Trigger events: agent go-live announcement, support-ops job posting mentioning AI tooling, new VP CX hire, funding round (scaling support), public refund-policy incident. Objections: §10. Expansion: second domain (discounts→credits→collections), seats, then Growth tier.

**Secondary ICP:** consumer-subscription/marketplace ops teams (ClassPass/Curology archetype) — credits are a *daily* decision domain; agent adoption is publicly proven; ACV smaller ($12–18K) but conversion faster. **Future ICP:** fintech mid-market billing-dispute teams (Y2, rides Annex III adjacency); enterprise internal-audit route (Y2+, per FOUNDER_BRAND_GTM §8).

## 7. Phase 6 — Outreach Playbook

**Cadence per account (14 days, then 60-day cooldown):** Day 0 email #1 → D2 LinkedIn connect (no note if <300 mutuals culture, else 1-liner) → D4 LinkedIn comment on their content (genuine) → D6 email #2 (new angle) → D9 LinkedIn DM → D12 email #3 (breakup). **Volume:** 20 new accounts/week ⇒ ~60 touches/week ⇒ sustainable solo. **Inbound engine:** runs via FOUNDER_BRAND_GTM (content → DMs); tag every inbound "how did you find me."

**10 cold-email angles** (subject → body core; personalize line 1 from evidence, always):
1. *Trigger:* "your {vendor} rollout — who signs off on the refunds?" → the audit question + free-report offer (the workhorse; full text in FOUNDER_PLAYBOOK §14).

## 8. Phase 7 — Discovery Framework

**Agenda (25 min):** 2' context/permission → 15' their world (questions below) → 5' mirror-back + one-screen demo *only if earned* → 3' next step (never end without one).

**50 questions by validation goal:**
*JTBD/workflow (1-10):* Walk me through the last refund that got escalated — start to finish. · Where does policy actually live? · Who wrote it, when was it last updated? · How does a new hire learn "how we decide"? · What % of decisions are judgment calls vs. rule-following? · When the agent (AI or human) is unsure, what happens? · How long does an escalation sit? · Who has final say on exceptions? · What's the workaround culture — Slack DMs? · Show me (screen-share) where a decision gets recorded today.
*Pain (11-20):* When did two people last decide the same case differently — cost? · What broke after your last policy change? · What's your ugliest refund story this year? · How much do you *think* inconsistency costs — gut number? · What does finance say about refund volume? · What did your last audit ask for, how long to assemble? · What keeps the {agent vendor} rollout from expanding to money-touching actions? · Who yells when a refund is wrong — and at whom? · What have you tried already? · Why did that fail?
*Buying process (21-28):* Who besides you would care? · Who could veto? · How did you buy your last ops tool — walk me through it. · What did it cost and who signed? · Security review: what does it involve here? · What would make this a Q3 vs. next-year conversation? · Budget line: does "decision governance" map to anything existing? · If the pilot works, what's the realistic path to paid?
*ROI (29-35):* Refund/credit volume per month? · Average decision value? · Escalation rate? · Time per audit-prep cycle? · Agent deflection rate today? · What would 20% fewer escalations be worth? · What metric does your VP get graded on?
*Pricing (36-40):* What do you pay for {Zendesk/Fin} today? · At what price is this a no-brainer / needs-VP / impossible? · Per-seat, per-domain, or flat — what fits how you budget? · What did the last tool you *churned* cost, and why'd it die? · Is free suspicious to you? (Listen hard.)
*Decision validation (41-45):* If I gave you the Leakage Report tomorrow, what would you do with it? · Who would you forward it to? · What number in it would make you act? · What would make this a "hell yes"? · What's the *real* reason this might go nowhere?
*Expansion (46-50):* Which decision domain hurts second-most? · Who owns discounts/credits/collections? · How many teams share this pain? · If this works for refunds, what next? · Who else in your network wrestles with this? (The referral ask.)

**Avoid:** "Would you use a tool that…" (hypothetical-yes trap) · "Don't you think agents need governance?" (leading) · "How much would you pay?" cold (anchor-free noise) · feature tours before pain · pitching in minute 3. **Real buying intent:** they screen-share their mess unprompted; they name a specific upcoming audit/rollout date; they introduce someone without being asked; they ask about security review process; they negotiate the program terms. **Fake interest:** "love the vision, keep me posted" · enthusiastic but won't share data · reschedules twice · asks only about the free part · "circle back after {vague event}."

## 9. Phase 8 — Demo Playbook

**Universal law:** demo *their* world, not your product. Pre-load the seeded tenant with 3 policies named like theirs (from discovery) before every demo. The one-screen kill shot, always the same: **change a policy → replay → "these 340 past decisions flip" → publish, signed.** Nobody in this market has seen that; it does more than 30 minutes of slides.

| Demo | Audience/goal | Flow (min) | Success = |
|---|---|---|---|
| First (post-discovery) | Champion; earn the pilot | 2 recap pain → 8 kill shot on mimicked policies → 5 shadow-mode "here's your version" → 5 program terms | Pilot agreement sent same day |
| Technical | IT/security; kill the blocker | Architecture (read-only, no-LLM-on-decisions, DPA, signed bundles verify endpoint) → data flow → retention/deletion | Security sign-off ≤2 weeks |
| Executive (VP/CFO) | Budget; 15 min max | 1 slide problem → leakage math *their numbers* → ledger-as-audit-artifact → price | Verbal budget yes |
| Champion enablement | Arm them to sell inside | Give: 3-slide deck, ROI one-pager, objection cheat-sheet | Champion presents without you |
| Pilot kickoff | All stakeholders | Success criteria on one page → data access → calendar of 12 weekly calls → Slack channel live | Data flowing ≤7 days |
| Pilot review (day 30/60/90) | Champion + VP (+CFO at 90) | Coverage stat → Leakage Report walkthrough → replay artifact → next-phase decision | Day-90: conversion conversation held |
| Expansion (post-paid) | Second domain owner | Their peer's results → second-domain shadow offer | Domain #2 pilot |

**Expected objections per demo:** first demo — "we're too busy this quarter" (answer: you do 90% of the work, their cost is one 45-min call/week); technical — "another vendor with our data" (read-only export, field-level minimization, deletion SLA, DPA); executive — "why not wait for {vendor} to add this" (self-attestation argument + neutrality); review — "interesting but not urgent" (tie to *their* named audit/rollout date from discovery, or accept the no).

## 11. Phase 10 — Pilot Program (the 90-day shadow)

**W1:** kickoff; data connection (Zendesk/Intercom export/API); Slack channel; success criteria signed. **W2-3:** founder encodes 10-20 policies with champion Q&A (this *is* discovery); first decisions shadowed. **W4:** coverage ≥60%; divergence findings preview. **Day 30 review:** **Leakage Report v1** — the wow moment. **W5-8:** coverage →90%; escalation loop live for ops lead; capture one *real* policy change → replay report on their history. **Day 60 review:** replay artifact + VP in the room. **W9-12:** audit-artifact pack (signed bundle, chain verification, trail export); CFO/finance walkthrough (week-8 commitment cashed in). **Day 90:** conversion conversation (below).
**KPIs:** coverage %, divergence rate, escalations adjudicated, replay runs, weekly-call attendance, time-to-encode per policy (<5 min target — arc DoD). **Feedback loop:** every call ends "what was confusing/missing/wrong this week?" → logged (§14). **Exit criteria (either side):** coverage <50% by day 45 (data problem), champion disengaged 3 weeks (org problem), value disproven honestly (thesis problem — *publish the learning*). **Conversion:** the day-90 meeting reviews the three artifacts against the day-0 success criteria; if met, the pre-agreed $750/mo begins day 121; if not met, extend 30 days once or part as friends with the report as parting gift.

## 12. Phase 11 — Pricing Experiments

**Sequence:** (1) Partners 1–3: free-90 → $750/mo pre-agreed (tests conversion willingness, not price level). (2) Partners 4–5: **paid pilot $500 flat for the 90 days** (tests whether *any* payment changes seriousness — Kazanjy's founder-sales insight: even token payment 10×'s engagement). (3) First non-partner deals: $1.5K/mo Team list — no discount, tests real WTP. (4) Month 6+: Growth tier $2.9K/mo when a partner wants domain #2 (expansion price test). (5) Enterprise pricing conversations only when *they* raise SSO/SOC2 (signal, not offer).
**Rejected models & why:** usage/per-decision (punishes adoption; wedge volumes too low — MARKET_VALIDATION §12) · per-seat primary (ledger value isn't seat-shaped; keep seats as tier boundary only) · pure-free tier now (attracts non-buyers, drains founder time) · annual-prepay pressure at this stage (kills trust-building; offer monthly, ask for annual only at renewal with a case study behind you).

## 13. Phase 12 — Customer Success (founder-led)

W1: kickoff ≤3 days from signature; data ≤7. W2: first policies live in shadow; champion can navigate console solo. W3: first divergence findings shared informally (don't wait for day 30 — early value = trust). M2: champion runs a replay unaided (activation moment); bi-weekly cadence. M3: finance walkthrough; conversion. **Delight levers:** respond in Slack in minutes (a solo founder's superpower — enjoy it while it lasts); surprise artifacts ("noticed your discount policy contradicts your refund policy — here's the diff"); remember and reference their exact horror stories. **Trust levers:** never let a bug reach them before your disclosure does; publish your own uptime/incidents honestly; say "I don't know, I'll find out" instead of improvising. **Founder-customer cadence:** weekly (M1) → bi-weekly (M2-3) → monthly + Slack (paid steady-state). Support: shared Slack, <24h SLA stated / <2h delivered.

## 14. Phase 13 — Product Feedback Loop

Single system (Linear or GitHub Projects — you live there anyway): every partner request logged with {partner, JTBD, frequency, revenue-weight, effort}. **Score = (partners requesting × urgency × conversion-impact) / effort.** Weekly 30-min solo triage (Fri); monthly roadmap note to all partners ("shipped / next / declined-and-why" — the *declined* section builds more trust than the shipped one). **Must build:** anything blocking coverage/activation at ≥2 partners (connectors, export formats). **Should build:** second-domain support when first partner asks to expand; Slack escalation notifications. **Ignore (say no kindly, in writing):** per-partner custom workflows, dashboards-for-dashboards, anything on the arc's Never list (execution, chat-product), single-partner requests contradicting the wedge (log for Y2). Rule from the arc discipline: **behavior-changing requests route through the same change-control rigor as your own ideas** — partners respect a vendor whose "no" has reasons.
