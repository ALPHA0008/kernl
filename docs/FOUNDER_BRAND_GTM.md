## 2. Phase 2 — Personal Brand vs Product Brand

**Answer: personal-first, 70/30, converging to 50/50 after design partners, company-led after seed.**

Reasoning:
- At 0 customers, nobody searches for "Kernl." They *will* read a founder with a sharp claim. Personal reach is the only distribution you can build with $0.
- **For a trust product, the founder's public credibility is literally part of the product.** Nobody adopts an audit ledger from an anonymous vendor; they adopt it from the person whose thinking on agent accountability they already respect. Your brand isn't marketing for the product — it *is* the early trust signal the product lacks (no SOC2, no logos yet).
- Product brand compounds later and transfers to employees/fundraising; personal brand is non-transferable but faster. Sequence accordingly.

**Roadmap:** Months 0–6: founder 70% (positioning + content engine + community standing), Kernl 30% (launch artifacts, design-partner case study). Months 6–12: 50/50 (case studies exist; company site/blog becomes canonical home). Post-seed: company-led 70%, founder becomes the *voice* of the category rather than the channel.

## 3. Phase 3 — Positioning

### You

**Own this niche: AI agent accountability.** Not "AI," not "agents," not "governance" broadly — the specific, visceral question: *"An AI agent just moved money. Who authorized that, and can you prove it?"*

- **One-line positioning:** *"I'm building the audit layer for the AI agent economy — the neutral ledger that proves which policy authorized what an agent did."*
- **What people should know you for:** the person who says, with receipts, that **agent governance without determinism is vibes**, and that vendor self-attestation will not survive contact with auditors.
- **Topics to dominate (5, no more):** (1) agent accountability & the coming audit reckoning; (2) deterministic policy vs. LLM-judged guardrails; (3) EU AI Act / Colorado operational reality for ops teams; (4) "policy is code" for refunds/discounts/credits (CI, replay, blast radius); (5) radically honest build-in-public engineering (your constitutional rules, your test discipline, your real numbers).
- **Banned labels:** AI Enthusiast · Builder · Serial founder · Visionary. Also banned: AGI takes, model-release hot takes, productivity-hack content — every off-niche post erodes the claim.

### Kernl

- **Category:** the **decision ledger** (own the term; long-run: the policy plane / Institutional Computing per `Product_summit.md`).
- **Positioning statement:** *"Sierra and Decagon are the agents doing the work. Kernl is the neutral system of record proving what policy authorized it — the audit layer no agent vendor can credibly provide for itself."*
- **Core differentiation:** deterministic (zero LLM on the decision path), replay-gated ("CI for your refund policy"), evidence-cited (no uncited norm), cryptographically signed, vendor-neutral.
- **Why customers care:** stop refund/discount leakage; make AI-agent rollouts audit-safe; change policy without breaking precedent.
- **Why investors care:** the neutral-ledger position in a market where execution-plane vendors ($15B Sierra, $4.5B Decagon) structurally cannot audit themselves; EU enforcement began 2026-08-02; corpus + switching-cost moat (see `MARKET_VALIDATION.md`).

## 4. Phase 4 — Personal Brand Strategy

**The founder you should become publicly: "the rigorous one."** In a feed full of agent hype, the person who ships deterministic systems, publishes real numbers, names hard trade-offs, and never overclaims. Think the credibility archetype of a Simon Willison / Gergely Orosz — earned through consistent, verifiable, technically-grounded publishing — pointed at one commercial niche.

**Perception to create:** *"If AI agents are going to touch money at my company, this is the person whose checklist I want."*

**Credibility mechanics (in order of power):** shipped artifacts (demos, specs, post-mortems) > specific numbers ("our evaluator is differential-tested against a Rust port across 5,000 generated cases") > named opinions ("self-attestation fails audit; here's why") > engagement volume. Never lead with follower-count games.

**Never do:** fake urgency or invented metrics (your whole brand is *auditability* — one caught embellishment is fatal); subtweet-fight Sierra/Decagon (punch at the *pattern*, self-attestation, never the company); engagement-bait ("Agree?"); AI-generated slop posts (your audience detects it and your product's honesty story dies); post off-niche.

**Founder story spine (use everywhere — bio, About, YC app, talks):** *"I watched AI agents get handed the power to move money with nothing but a prompt between them and a company's bank account. Everyone was racing to make agents smarter; nobody was building the thing that proves what they were authorized to do. So I built the boring, rigorous layer: a decision ledger where policy is code — versioned, replay-tested, signed. Because in five years, 'an agent did it' won't be an excuse that survives an audit."*

## 8. Phase 8 — Enterprise Sales Strategy

**Will they take you seriously?** Today: no — and that's correct. A solo pre-revenue vendor selling *governance* to a Fortune-1000 is a contradiction. **You don't need them yet** (`FOUNDER_PLAYBOOK.md` sequences enterprise at Y2). What earns the meeting later: mid-market lighthouse logos + SOC2 Type I + the neutral-auditor narrative + a champion who moved companies.

**The ladder (2027+):** (1) Land through the *internal audit / risk* door, not IT — they have the "prove what the agents did" mandate and smaller vendor-risk allergies for read-only shadow deployments. (2) **Paid pilot always** — $15–25K/quarter, defined success criteria (e.g., "ledger covers 95% of refund decisions in BU X; audit team signs off on trail quality"). Free enterprise pilots signal worthlessness and die in procurement anyway. (3) De-risk mechanically: shadow-mode (no workflow change), read-only ingestion, SOC2, DPA, your determinism story ("no LLM touches your decisions" is an enterprise *superpower*), and the signed-bundle verification endpoint (they can verify your math without trusting you — unique). (4) Pilot→contract: pre-negotiate the conversion in the pilot agreement (pilot fee credits toward year one), keep the champion armed with a one-page ROI memo written for their CFO, and land as a departmental purchase (<$75K) before attempting platform status.

## 14. Phase 14 — Mistakes to Avoid

**Time-wasters:** daily posting for its own sake (3 sharp > 7 mid); follower-count vanity (1,000 support-ops leaders beat 50K AI tourists); building a Discord/community before having customers; conference *sponsoring* pre-seed; polishing the product further (it is done enough — the repo is your comfort zone, the calendar is the job).
**Branding mistakes:** positioning drift (one niche, 12 months minimum); AI-generated content (fatal for an auditability brand); overclaiming ("first/only/revolutionary"); vague inspiration posts.
**Content mistakes:** posting without a claim; hiding the product (you're not "building something cool in stealth" — you're building Kernl, say it); ignoring comments (the algorithm and the relationships both live there); never asking (the 20% is where partners come from).
**Networking mistakes:** pitching on first contact; mass connection blasts; collecting advisors for logo value; asking busy people for "a quick call to pick your brain" (ask something specific and answerable in writing).
**GTM mistakes:** parallelizing channels before one works; discounting to $0 for enterprise; selling determinism when the buyer wants blame-protection; waiting for the product to be "ready" (it is).
**Intentionally ignore:** YouTube grind, Product Hunt (for now), Medium/Dev.to, growth hacks, follower-loop pods, AGI discourse, competitor drama.
